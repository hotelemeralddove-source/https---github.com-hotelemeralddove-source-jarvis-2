THE HOLOGRAM DECK

A scientific server running, projected in front of you: a dotted wireframe planet, a stack of tilted light rings spiralling into a bright core, a numeric data field, five live instrument panes, two amber byte counts, a palette block, a marquee of measured integers, a 12-second telemetry spectrum, per-core load bars and a real log ticker. Say "show me the hologram" — or click Hologram deck in the tray — and it takes the screen. Esc gives it back.

1 · The same surface, inside the magic screen

The deck is not a separate toy: it is the wallpaper of the chat surface. The app buttons and the input box float on top of it, and the instrument panes stay out of their way (the globe is raised and shortened in this mode).

3 · The rules it is built under

  • No invented numbers. Nothing on the panel is random. With no psutil the panes say no sensor instead of a guess. The build fails if a random() call appears in the telemetry path — tests/test_holo.py checks that, and reads the frame back with the app's own OCR to prove the labels are legible.
  • Projector artefacts are labelled. Grain, flicker and the sweep are a simulation of a projector, scaled by the theme's effect level — off in Gaming mode, where the deck is skipped entirely (effects < 0.2).
  • Free and light. No OpenGL, no shaders, no GPU requirement: cached layers and a texture brush, 81 MB RSS for the whole app.
  • Reversible. It is a window, not a shell replacement. Esc closes it; the Windows taskbar is never touched.
