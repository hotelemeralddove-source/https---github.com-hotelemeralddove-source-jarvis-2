Hotel management

say: occupancy 24 rooms · 17 sold · 42500 room revenue · 26000 costs
Occupancy: 70.8%   (rooms sold 17 ÷ 24 available)
ADR: 2,500.00   (room revenue ÷ rooms sold)
RevPAR: 1,770.83   (room revenue ÷ rooms available)
TRevPAR: 2,145.83   (all revenue ÷ rooms available)
GOPPAR: 1,062.50   ((revenue − costs) ÷ rooms available)
Total gross margin: 49.5%

Read them in this order: occupancy, then ADR, then RevPAR. Chasing occupancy with discounts raises the first and lowers the third — RevPAR is the one that pays salaries.
Rule of thumb worth checking against your own books: dropping ADR by 10% needs roughly 11% more rooms sold just to stand still.

Mentor

say: invert: get 20 direct bookings
Inversion · get 20 direct bookings

Instead of "how do I achieve get 20 direct bookings?", answer: **how would I guarantee failure?**

  1. What would make get 20 direct bookings impossible in 30 days? (list five, honestly)
  2. Which of those am I already doing, a little?
  3. What is the cheapest way to stop the top one this week?

Why this works: the list of ways to fail is usually shorter, more concrete, and more
actionable than the list of ways to succeed. Avoiding stupidity is easier than
seeking brilliance — and it compounds faster.
